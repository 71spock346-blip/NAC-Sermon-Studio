Back to the app

The Study Bible is a personal tool for reading Scripture, keeping your own thoughts on it, seeing where the Catechism of the New Apostolic Church refers to a verse, and preparing a divine service from a text word. Everything you write stays on your own device. There is no account and no server.

Reading

  • Find a passage: choose the book and chapter at the top, or type a reference such as Ps 130:7, Lukas 8:16 or 1 Kor 13 in the "Go to" box. English and Afrikaans book names both work. The ‹ and › buttons turn the chapter; on a PC the left and right arrow keys do the same.
  • Translations: the King James Version (with the Apocrypha) and the World English Bible are built in and work offline. Choose the translation next to the Go to box.
  • The Aa button holds the reading choices: the text size (the largest, "Pulpit", is meant for reading while standing), the look of the app (System, Paper, White or Dark), "Compare", which shows a second translation under each verse, and "Beside", which opens the same chapter from BibleSA, the Bible Society of South Africa's free site with the Afrikaans Bibles, in its own window next to the app; while that window stays open it follows you from chapter to chapter. (BibleSA does not allow its pages to be shown inside another site, so a window is the closest the app can come.)
  • Your own text: for a Bible text you may use yourself, tick its version under Settings → Translations → "My pasted text", choose it in the reader (marked "pasted") and paste each chapter in from the page where you read it, verse numbers included. It stays in this browser only and has its own save file in Settings.
  • NKJV and Afrikaans: these are copyrighted and cannot be built in. Add your own free key for API.Bible or the YouVersion Platform in Settings to read them inside the app (see below), or use the links under each chapter to open the same chapter on YouVersion, on BibleSA or on BibleGateway.
  • Marks: a purple C means the Catechism refers to that verse; tap it to see which sections. A pen means you have a note on that verse.

Appearance

Under Settings → Appearance (and under the Aa button in the reader) you choose the look: System follows the dark or light setting of your phone or PC, Paper is the warm default, White is plain, and Dark is for reading at night. The same place sets the text size and whether book names are shown in English or Afrikaans. Each device keeps its own choice.

Notes

  • Tap a verse (or several) in the reader and choose Note. Give it a kind: a thought, an experience, a doctrine link, an illustration, a sermon idea or a question. Tags are optional.
  • The Notes tab lists everything, and searches by word, tag or reference.
  • Experiences and illustrations can be inserted into a sermon preparation with one tap.

Catechism

The Catechism tab lists the Catechism's chapters and sections. Open a section to see the Bible verses it quotes. The text of the sections is the Church's copyright, so it is not part of the public app: load it once on each device under Settings → Catechism, from the file catechism-text.json that is made from the Catechism PDF (python3 bible/tools/build_catechism.py catechism.txt catechism-toc.txt --text catechism-text.json, where catechism.txt is the PDF as text from pdftotext -layout). It stays in this browser on this device and is not in the backup file.

With the text loaded you can read every section in the Catechism tab, with the book's own summary at the top and "see 4.2" links between sections; search the whole Catechism by word; tap the C on a verse to see the summary of each section that quotes it; and open a section under a service preparation with "Read". Without it, each section links to the same text at nak.org.

Preparing a divine service

  1. Select the text word in the reader and choose Prepare a service, or tap "New preparation" in the Prepare tab.
  2. Fill in the date, congregation and occasion. The text can be fetched from a translation or typed in.
  3. Write the message and core thought from the Divine Service Guide in your own words, then your understanding of the text: context, key words, the Catechism sections that quote it, cross references. Tick the Catechism sections to keep and choose what the sermon notes should carry for them: the full text of each section, the Catechism's own summary, or the number and title only.
  4. Build the sermon: introduction, main points, application for the congregation, conclusion, illustrations.
  5. Save or print the sermon notes (everything, A4) or the cue card (one A5 page in large type). On a phone, "Share" sends the PDF to WhatsApp or e-mail.
  6. After the service, add a short reflection. It is saved with the preparation.

Everything saves as you type. Use the status (Draft, Ready, Held) to keep the list tidy.

Keys for the licensed translations

Two services license Bible translations to apps, and both give a free key for personal, non-commercial use. API.Bible: open scripture.api.bible/signup, create an application and copy its key; it carries the NKJV. YouVersion Platform: register at platform.youversion.com, create an app and copy its app key; YouVersion carries the Afrikaans Bibles. Paste a key under Settings → Translations and tap "Check key": it lists the Bibles that key may read, and you tick the ones to show in the reader. Which translations appear depends on the licences each service has granted your key. YouVersion also shows the Bibles it carries in your languages that are not licensed to your app yet, marked "licence needed": tap Licence next to one to see which agreement covers it, accept that agreement on platform.youversion.com, then check the key again. Keys are stored only on your device and are never included in a backup.

Backup and devices

Notes and preparations live in the browser on each device. Under Settings → Backup, Save backup file makes one file with everything; keep it in your Google Drive. Load backup file on another device merges it in: newer copies win and nothing is deleted. Clearing the browser's site data erases the app's storage, so save a backup first.

Offline and home screen

The app keeps every chapter you open. To have the whole Bible ready without a connection, use Download all texts for offline in Settings once. On a phone, use the browser's "Add to Home Screen" to open it like an app.
