Help

Back to the app

The Study Bible is a personal tool for reading Scripture, keeping your own thoughts on it, seeing where the Catechism of the New Apostolic Church refers to a verse, and preparing a divine service from a text word. Everything you write stays on your own device. There is no account and no server.

Start here

A new device asks these five things itself the first time the app opens; this is the same list. Settings → About has the five steps again, under Set up this device again.

  1. The language. EN or AF at the top of the app: the screens, the buttons, this help page and the book names.
  2. Your name and congregation. The name goes in the footer of the sermon notes, and the congregation is filled in for each new preparation (Settings → You).
  3. A sync code, or a fresh start. If you use the Study Bible on another device already, type its code (Settings → Sync → Show the code, on that device) and your notes and preparations come over to this one. Otherwise start fresh; a code can be typed in later.
  4. Church files. The Catechism, the altar booklet, the handbooks and the month's Guide are the Church's own and not part of the app: load each once from its file under Church files. A minister who has them can send you the files from his app.
  5. Add to Home Screen. The app offers it in the last step and under Settings → Offline; where the browser makes no offer, its own menu has it. From the home screen the app opens like any other, works without the network, and what you save is kept safe.

Finding your way

The app opens on Sunday: the next divine service, with the date, the text word, the title and how far your preparation is, and one tap to each thing you use on the day: the cue cards, the sermon, the altar card of the Liturgy and the text word in large letters. On the day itself, Altar mode on the card puts the cue cards and the altar card on one full screen, a swipe apart, with the sermon after them when it is written, in large letters and with the screen kept awake; the ✕ at the top brings you back. Sunday pack under the card prints the cue cards, with the hymn numbers at the top of the first, and the altar card as one PDF of upright A5 pages, in one go; on A4 paper choose 2 pages per sheet in the print window. A service of a loaded Divine Service Guide that has no preparation yet is there too, with one tap to start it. After the service, tap Mark as held and the next service takes its place; the two services after it are listed under the card.

Service record. After the service, tap Service record (Record in a phone's bar). It opens the record of the service just held: today's, or else the last one. Which service at the top goes to another; the Sunday card's Service record and the preparation's Print step open it too. NACSA's form is on one screen: the congregation, date, text word and kind of service come from the preparation, the hymns and your name are filled in, the attendance and the offerings add themselves up, and there is room for the Apostle's sealings and ordinations, the remarks, who completed the record and who captured it on MIS. What you type is kept as you go. In the hymn boxes the keyboard has letters: type the book before the number, A 104 (Afrikaans), E 104 (English) or HC 12 (the Hymn Collection). Under The form you see your record on NACSA's paper form, as the office knows it (in English, like the paper). Send as a picture sends it like a photo of the form: choose WhatsApp, then the district's group. Send as PDF does the same with a PDF, and Two on one A4 (PDF) gives two forms side by side on one sheet, like the paper. Under The message, Send to WhatsApp opens WhatsApp with the record as typed lines, and Copy copies them.

On a PC every part of the app is in the blue menu on the left; Seating, Choir, Reminders, MIS and Reflection evening stand together there under the heading Tools. On a phone the bar at the bottom holds Read, Prepare, Sunday, Notes and Record (the service record), and a sixth tab, More, lists the rest: Handbooks, Theology, Month plan, the Tools under their heading, Church files and Settings. While one of those is open, More stays lit, so a tap on it brings the list back. The app opens on Sunday, the next service.

Handbooks is the door to the Church's books: the Catechism, the Creed, the Liturgy (the words at the altar) and the handbooks for ministers, each a row in its list. While the Catechism or the Liturgy is open, Handbooks stays lit on a PC, and More on a phone, so a tap on it brings the list back.

Settings is a list of sections, each with a line on what is inside; tap one to open it on its own, and ‹ Settings above it brings the list back. The sections a new minister needs come first, under Start here: Language, You, What I use, Sync, Church files and Translations. Then what belongs to this device: Appearance, Backup, Offline, the files of the old apps and About. The keys for the voice and for Claude come last. Wherever this page says "Settings → Language", it means that row.

What I use, under Settings → Start here, is a tick list of the five tools: Month plan, Seating, Choir, Reminders and Reflection evening. What you untick leaves the menu on a PC and the More page on a phone, on all your devices; nothing is deleted, and a tick brings it back. A deacon's phone need not show the rector's and the conductor's tools.

Church files, in the menu on a PC and under More on a phone, is where the Church's own texts are loaded, each once from its own file: the text of the Catechism (from the Catechism's PDF), the altar booklet in English and in Afrikaans, the handbooks for ministers and the Divine Service Guide. A row per file says whether it is on this device, with one Load button and, once it is loaded, Remove. What is loaded reaches your other devices through the sync and is not in the backup file.

The Guide's row works for every minister. Where the AI helper is unlocked, Load a Guide (PDF) has Claude read the month's booklet; then Send this month's Guide makes a small file of it, in every language loaded, and on a phone opens the share sheet with WhatsApp in it (on a PC the file is saved, to send by hand). The minister who gets it taps Load a Guide file here, or on the Guide's card under Prepare, and picks the file: the services, the articles and the main points are then on that device too, with no key and no Claude. On a phone where the app is installed, WhatsApp's Share on the file lists the app itself, and the Guide loads at once. The Guide goes from one minister's device to another's and never through the site.

MIS, under Tools, opens the Church's own MIS site. In the installed app it opens over the app: on a phone with a Done or ✕ at the top to come back, on a PC in the app's window with a bar at the top whose ✕ brings the app back. In a browser it opens in a tab of its own. Sign in there as always; the app never sees or keeps your password. The MIS cannot sit inside the app's own page: it signs you in with a cookie, which a browser holds back inside another site's page, so the sign-in went through but the MIS could not see it.

Language

The app speaks English or Afrikaans. The EN / AF switch sits in the header on a phone and at the foot of the side menu on a PC, and the same choice is under Settings → Language and under the Aa button in the reader. It changes the screens, the buttons and this help page. The headings of the sermon notes, of "The sermon" and of the cue cards (Introduction, Main points and so on), with the date and the kind of service, follow the language of the sermon, which each preparation chooses under Who is listening; "As usual" is the app's language. It also sets the book names and the language of Claude's suggestions, and each of those can be changed on its own afterwards under Settings. The Bible text itself stays in the translation you choose.

Reading

  • Open a passage: choose the book and chapter at the top. On a phone the bar is one row: tap the chapter's name (Psalm 23) to pick a book and a chapter from a grid, with the chapters before and after at the top; the bar slides away as you read down and comes back when you scroll up. The ‹ and › buttons turn the chapter, at the top on a PC and at the end of the chapter on a phone; on a PC the left and right arrow keys do the same.
  • Find a verse: the box at the top (on a phone, behind the magnifier) takes a place or words. Type a place such as Ps 130:7, Lukas 8:16 or 1 Kor 13 and the chapter opens there. English and Afrikaans book names both work, and a small slip in a name, such as Jhon 3:16, is understood. Or type a few words you remember, such as shepherd want. The app lists the verses that have them, with the verses that have the words close together first. Tap a verse to open it; "Back to the verses" above the chapter returns to the list. A spelling slip is put right where it can be, and the page says which word was searched. Put words in "quotes" to find them side by side, exactly as typed.
  • What is searched: the translation the reader is set to, and the page always says which one. Type the words in the language of that translation. The KJV and the WEB are searched on the device. The first search fetches the whole Bible, which takes a moment and needs a connection, unless you saved it with "Download all texts for offline". After that the search works without a connection. A text you pasted in is searched in the chapters you pasted. The NKJV is searched by API.Bible with your key, so it needs a connection; when that does not work, the KJV is searched and the page says so.
  • Translations: the King James Version (with the Apocrypha) and the World English Bible are built in and work offline. Choose the translation in the bar at the top.
  • The Aa button holds the reading choices: the text size (the largest, "Pulpit", is meant for reading while standing), the look of the app (System, White, Paper or Dark), the letters and spacing ("Easy-read" uses plain letters with more room between them and between the lines, in the whole app, which many people with dyslexia find easier), the language of the app, "Compare", which shows a second translation under each verse, and "Beside", which opens the same chapter from BibleSA, the Bible Society of South Africa's free site with the Afrikaans Bibles, in its own window next to the app; while that window stays open it follows you from chapter to chapter. (BibleSA does not allow its pages to be shown inside another site, so a window is the closest the app can come.)
  • Your own text: for a Bible text you may use yourself, choose its version in the reader's translation list (its short name, such as AFR83, under "Paste it in yourself"). An empty chapter shows Open on BibleSA and Paste the chapter: copy the chapter there, with its verse numbers, come back and tap Paste; it is taken from the clipboard and kept at once, with Undo in the message. It stays in this browser only and has its own save file under Settings → Translations → "My pasted text".
  • NKJV and Afrikaans: these are copyrighted and cannot be built in. Add your own free key for API.Bible or the YouVersion Platform in Settings to read them inside the app (see below), or use the links under each chapter to open the same chapter on YouVersion, on BibleSA or on BibleGateway.
  • Marks: a purple C means the Catechism refers to that verse; tap it to see which sections. A pen means you have a note on that verse.

Appearance

The app wears the Church's blue and white, like the slides: the blue header with the emblem on a phone, the blue side menu on a PC, and every letter in Montserrat. Under Settings → Appearance (and under the Aa button in the reader) you choose the look: White is the blue and white of the slides, Paper keeps the blue but gives the pages a warm cream colour, Dark is for reading at night, and System is White by day and Dark at night, as your phone or PC is set. The same place sets the text size, the letters and spacing (Montserrat, or Easy-read: plain letters in the whole app with more room between them) and whether book names are shown in English or Afrikaans. Each device keeps its own choice.

Notes

  • Tap a verse (or several) in the reader and choose Note. Give it a kind: a thought, an experience, a doctrine link, an illustration, a sermon idea or a question. Tags are optional.
  • The Notes tab lists everything, and searches by word, tag or reference.
  • Listen: reads a note aloud with the voice built into your phone or PC, free and at once, in the note's language (English or Afrikaans, when the device has such a voice; Settings → Read aloud says which it has). There you can choose ElevenLabs' cheapest voice for notes instead; that recording stays on the device, so hearing it again costs nothing.
  • Each card shows the verse, the kind and the note's own words first; the date, Listen and Edit sit under them, so a list of fifty notes reads down like a page.
  • English | Afrikaans: a note that has both languages shows an EN | AF switch under its words: flip between them, also offline and on your other devices. Edit changes the language you see. Bible verses quoted from a translation stay as they are. A note in one language shows no switch.
  • To have a note in the other language too, open Edit and tap Translate into Afrikaans (or English): Claude translates it once (this needs your Anthropic key, under Settings → AI helper). The other language stays as it is, and says so when the note changed after it was translated.
  • Experiences and illustrations can be inserted into a sermon preparation with one tap, in the preparation's language when the note has it.

Catechism

Catechism, the first row under Handbooks, lists the Catechism's chapters and sections. Open a section to see the Bible verses it quotes. The text of the sections is the Church's copyright, so it is not part of the public app: load it once under Church files, straight from the Church's own PDF of the Catechism (Load the Catechism (PDF)). The app reads the sections out of the PDF on the device itself, page by page, and matches them to the built-in index; it takes a minute or so. The text stays on your devices (it reaches the others through the sync) and is not in the backup file. A catechism-text.json made earlier with bible/tools/build_catechism.py loads there too.

With the text loaded you can read every section under Catechism, with the book's own summary at the top and "see 4.2" links between sections; search the whole Catechism by word; tap the C on a verse to see the summary of each section that quotes it; and open a section under a service preparation with "Read". Without it, each section links to the same text at nak.org. The Catechism's text and index are in English, so search it with English words. With the text loaded, Print on a section opens "Print or save": the section, or the section with those under it, as a PDF in large clear type, with Save as PDF, Print and, on a phone, Share.

Ask the Catechism: where the AI helper is unlocked and the text is loaded, a box under the heading takes a question in your own words. Claude reads the sections that fit it and answers from them in plain words, naming the sections, each a link, so you can read what the Catechism itself says. The answer is not kept. The same question can be put under Handbooks, where the Catechism is read together with the handbooks.

Theology

Theology gives the background to the Bible at three levels: the verses you choose, the chapter, and the book with its writer. On a PC it has its own place in the side menu; on a phone it is under More. You also reach it from the reader: the Theology button beside the chapter's title opens that chapter, and after you tap verses, Theology in the bar at the bottom opens a sheet on those verses, with the chapter and the book a tap away.

Every book of the Bible has a short introduction built in, the later writings of the Old Testament (the Apocrypha) included, in English and in Afrikaans: who wrote it, when, for whom and why, its big themes, how it is built, its key verses and how it points to Christ. The books are grouped as the Catechism groups them, and beside each book, chapter and passage stand the Catechism sections that quote it. The introductions are written for the app in plain words: a help for study, not the Church's doctrine.

With your Anthropic key (see "Help from Claude" below), Ask Claude writes the background of the verses, the chapter or the book: what it says in plain words, its setting and writer, the key words, what the Church teaches on it, how other Christians read it (each reading marked as in line with New Apostolic teaching, different from it, or left open by the Catechism), how it points to Christ, other passages, and a thought for today. With the Catechism text loaded on the device, Claude says what the Church teaches from the Catechism itself; without it, Claude only names the sections. It takes about a minute, and the cost shows with the answer. What Claude wrote is kept on the device, goes into the backup file and travels with the sync. Ask again writes it anew and keeps the earlier answer under the new one: tap it to read it, or tap Put this one first. Nothing Claude wrote goes unless you tap Remove, which asks first and takes away only that one answer (Undo brings it back).

English | Afrikaans: every answer has an EN | AF switch at its top. Tap the other language and Claude translates the answer once (the cost shows when it is done). After that you flip between the two, also offline and on your other devices, and the list under Theology shows each answer in the app's language when it has one. When the answer in the first language changes later, the translation says so and offers Translate again; the earlier translation stays under the new one. Ask again and Remove work on the language you see.

Under what Claude wrote, Save all of this keeps everything the page shows: the verses (for a book, its introduction), all that Claude wrote and the Catechism sections. Save as PDF gives a file to keep, for example in your Google Drive, or to send. Print prints it, and on a phone Share sends it straight to another app. Save as a note puts all of it in one of your notes, as a Doctrine note on that place, and you can still change it before you save it.

Liturgy

Liturgy holds the Church's altar booklet, Liturgy for Altar Use: the words said at the altar in the divine service and in each act of blessing (adoption, confirmation, a wedding, a funeral, the blessing before a birth, a wedding anniversary, Holy Baptism), one card per rite, as the booklet prints them: the label on the left, the words on the right, the booklet's notes in italics and its lists. It is a row under Handbooks, on a PC and on a phone, and on a phone it sits at the top of Prepare as well.

The booklet is the Church's own, so it is not part of the app. Load its PDF once under Church files: Load it (PDF) on the row of the English booklet or of the Afrikaans one, or both. The app reads the cards from the file on the device itself (no Claude, no key, a few seconds), keeps them in this browser and sends them to your other devices through the sync, so one load is enough. They are not in the backup file. Under Church files, Remove takes a booklet off your devices again (Undo brings it back), and the PDF can be loaded anew at any time; Load a newer file replaces a booklet.

The cards show in the app's language, or in the other language when only that booklet is loaded, and a card has an EN | AF switch when both are loaded. Aa sets the text size, the same sizes as in the reader, up to Pulpit. While a card is open the phone keeps its screen on, so the words stay at the altar.

Print on a card opens "Print or save": the card as a PDF, the label on the left and the words on the right as the booklet prints them, on A4 or on A5, the size of the cards at the altar. A card is fitted on one page, in the largest letters that fit there, and the line under the paper you choose says so. Only a card too long to be read at that size takes a second page; a card that needs two pages on A5 often fits on one page of A4. A5, one side keeps such a card on one side of an A5 card all the same, in smaller letters, and the line says what size they are (8 point for the Divine Service). To print A5 cards on A4 paper, choose 2 pages per sheet under "More settings" in the print window: each card then comes out at its own size, half the sheet. With 4 pages per sheet a card is only a quarter of the sheet, and its letters come out almost a third smaller still. Save as PDF keeps the file, for example in your Google Drive, Print sends it to the printer, and on a phone Share sends it to another app, as under "Print or save" in Prepare. Print at the top of the list gives all the cards of the booklet, each fitted on a page of its own. On a PC, Ctrl+P on a card prints that same PDF.

A card's words can be changed by hand, when the Church changes a wording or the booklet has a misprint: open the card, tap Edit, change the words in the boxes (the label on the left, each paragraph on the right; a note stays a note), and tap Save. Enter starts a new paragraph, a box left empty takes its words away, ▲ ▼ move a row up or down, ✕ takes a row away and Add a row adds one at the end. The changed card is kept on your devices like the booklet, and the list marks it "Changed by you". Back to the booklet's words under the card puts the booklet's own words back. Each language is changed on its own: switch to AF and change the Afrikaans card too. A booklet loaded anew from its PDF brings the file's words back on every card.

The full Liturgy book of 2024, with the long form of the divine service and every act of blessing, is a handbook (below); a line at the foot of the Liturgy list leads to it.

Handbooks

Handbooks holds the Church's Creed and its handbooks for ministers, to read and to search. The Creed, the ten Articles of Faith, is part of the app, in English and in Afrikaans (the EN | AF switch on its pages shows the other): it opens whole on one page, article by article, each with a link to the chapter of the Catechism that explains it (2.4), and it is searched and asked like the handbooks. The handbooks are the Guide for Ministers (the rule book: ministry, the divine service, the sacraments and acts of blessing, pastoral care, administration, the competency profiles), the "Serving and Leading" guiding principle, and the Liturgy of the New Apostolic Church of 2024. Another handbook in the same shape, with numbered chapters, can be loaded as well. The Catechism and the Liturgy (the altar cards) are rows in the list too, the Catechism first, each opening its own screen; once the Catechism's text is loaded under Church files, its sections are searched and asked together with the handbooks. On a PC Handbooks is in the side menu after Prepare; on a phone it is the fourth tab of the bar.

The handbooks are the Church's own, so they are not part of the app. Load each one once under Church files, as its PDF or as a text file saved from the PDF: Load it (PDF or text file) under its name. The app reads the chapters from the file on the device itself (no Claude, no key, a few seconds for a PDF), keeps them in this browser and sends them to your other devices through the sync, so one load is enough. They are not in the backup file. Under Church files each handbook has a row with its edition and when it was loaded; Remove takes it off your devices again (Undo brings it back) and Load a newer file replaces it. A handbook loaded in English and in Afrikaans has an EN | AF switch on its pages.

The list opens a handbook's Contents: its chapters by number. A chapter shows its text as the book prints it (lists, notes, the Liturgy's binding texts in italics), the chapters under it, and ‹ previous and next › at the foot; Aa sets the text size, the same sizes as in the reader. The search box on the Handbooks page finds every chapter across the loaded handbooks that carries the words you type, with a snippet; on a handbook's own page the search stays within that book and takes a chapter number too ("4.5").

Print on a chapter opens "Print or save": the chapter as a PDF in large clear type, on its own or with the chapters under it; on a handbook's Contents page the whole handbook; on the Creed the whole Creed, and on one of its articles that article; on a Catechism section the section, or the section with those under it. Save as PDF keeps the file, for example in your Google Drive, Print sends it to the printer, and on a phone Share sends it to another app, the same buttons as under "Print or save" in Prepare. The PDF is in the language the page shows: a handbook in Afrikaans prints in Afrikaans. On a PC, Ctrl+P prints the page you are reading on its own, a chapter or a section, without the menu around it; on an altar card it prints the card's own PDF, fitted on one page.

Where the AI helper is unlocked on the device, Ask the handbooks on the Handbooks page takes a question in your own words ("How long may the sermon be?"). Claude reads the chapters that fit the question and answers from them in plain words, naming the chapters, so the answer can be checked against the book; the answer is shown, not kept, and its cost shows with it.

The Catechism in questions and answers. The Church's Catechism in 750 questions and answers (in Afrikaans Kategismus van die Nuwe Apostoliese Kerk in vrae en antwoorde, 2015) loads under Church files from its own PDF, in the row after the Catechism. The app reads the questions on the device itself, with no key. It is then listed under Handbooks right after the Catechism: its Contents are the Catechism's 13 chapters, a chapter lists its questions, and a question shows its answer, the Bible verses it quotes in full with their references, the terms it explains in small type, the verses as buttons that open the reader, and the questions it points to under See also. The search finds a question by a word or by its number ("108"), and on the Catechism's own tab a section links to the questions on its chapter. In Prepare, step 5 lists the questions that quote the text word under the Catechism references: tick one and it goes into the sermon notes like a Catechism section, whole, its first paragraph or its number and question alone, as "In the sermon notes" says.

Seating and communion

Seating is the congregation's seating and communion plan: the floor plan of the building, the ministers put into the numbered seats, who stands at each serving station, and the A4 sheet that comes out of it for WhatsApp. Gezina, Sinoville and Hercules are drawn in; any other congregation is added under Layout.

Tap a numbered square on the plan and choose a minister, or type a name. Tap a second seat and the two change places. The hatched squares are the serving stations: tap one and choose the seat number of the minister who stands there. Under the plan, Seats is the same thing as a table, Communion holds who serves whom and the cup pairs, and sets how many stations each group has and how many cups stand on the altar.

In Roster each minister is given the colour of the section they serve. That colour then follows them: to their seat, to the station carrying their seat number, into the tables and onto the sheet. Several ministers may share a colour. Every layout names its own sections and chooses their colours.

Seats are numbered to one standard, so a plan reads the same wherever it comes from. Seat 2 is the presiding minister, whoever is serving: the Apostle, District Apostle or Chief Apostle. Their seat is on the bench to the left of the altar, second from the congregation end, with seat 1 beside them closest to the congregation. Along a bench the numbers count up from the congregation end towards the altar, and the benches fill from the one nearest the altar outwards.

A plan is kept only once you tap Save plan; saved plans are listed under Plans. The plan you are working on stays on the device you are working on, so nobody is ever editing your half-finished plan, but the congregations' layouts, the saved plans and the roster go to your other devices through the sync and sit in the backup file. That is the difference from the app this used to be: a layout traced on a PC is on the phone and on another minister's devices without anyone sending a file.

Layout is where a congregation is drawn. New layout from this one copies the one in front of you; Edit this layout turns the plan into an editor where every block, seat, marker, the altar and each station group is dragged into place and given its label, size, colour or direction; Open a layout file or a picture takes a layout someone sent, or a photograph of a plan to trace over. Editing a built-in congregation keeps your own copy of it, and Put the built-in version back undoes that.

Choir programme

Choir is the conductor's work: the hymn index of 1317 entries, the music programme for a divine service, the timed before-service running order, the festive service preparation form and the practices leading up to it. Each sheet is drawn as the workbook printed it and can be saved as a PDF, shared to WhatsApp or printed.

The service's details are at the top, and the six panes under them are the sheets. In Programme, type a hymn number such as 104, E 104 or A 12, or tap Find to search the index by title. Before counts the running order backwards from the start of the service, so the closing silence ends as the service begins and every time moves when one duration changes. Hymns is the index itself, with the conductor's own ability ratings; Files keeps the programmes written so far.

Each programme is kept separately, so two devices that each write one keep both, and all of them travel with the sync and sit in the backup file.

Birthdays and anniversaries

Reminders is the congregation's birthdays and wedding anniversaries, with a WhatsApp greeting ready for each person. Under Listings, load the Birthday Listing and Marriage Anniversary Listing PDFs that the church office prints: every member on them comes onto the list with their member number, section, date and contact number. The files are read on your device and go nowhere. A member already on the list is updated, not added again, so a fresh listing can be loaded every month, and the listing's age or years married is kept as a year of birth or marriage, so the counts stay right in later years.

Coming up shows the dates in the next weeks: today's first, then the days ahead, with a round birthday or anniversary marked as a milestone. A husband and wife on the same anniversary are one line. Tap Message and choose a wording: each is filled in with the person's name, the number they are turning or the years they are married, and your name as the sender, in English or Afrikaans, with or without a verse. Change the words if you like, then Open in WhatsApp opens the chat with the message already typed, so you only press send. Mark as sent remembers it; sent marks travel with the sync, so two ministers greeting from one list see what the other has done. Where a member has no number, Copy message lets you paste it elsewhere.

Congregants is the whole list, searchable by name, member number or section. Tap a member to correct their WhatsApp number, give them the name they go by, or add a note that is shown whenever you write to them. Only those without a number narrows the list to the members the office has no number for, and Export them to Excel makes a spreadsheet of them to send to the priest who knows them. When it comes back filled in, load it under Listings: only the WhatsApp number, preferred name and notes columns are read, matched on the member number, so nothing else in the sheet can change the list.

Visits. On the same member sheet you note your pastoral visits: tap Visited today after a visit, write a line on what was said or what to follow up, and plan the next visit by date or with a tap, In a month. The member's line under Congregants then shows the last and the next visit, and Visited longest ago first orders the list by who has waited longest. When a planned visit's day comes, or has passed, the member is listed under Visits due at the top of Coming up. Earlier visits are kept under the member, twelve at most. These notes are yours: they stay on your devices and in the sync, like the list itself, and never go to the site.

Under Settings on this tab you sign the greetings, choose their language, how far ahead the list looks and, where several ministers share one congregation, which section's dates you see. Save calendar file writes every birthday and anniversary as a yearly appointment with an alarm: open the file once and your phone's calendar goes off each year on its own, whether or not the app is open, with the member's WhatsApp link in the appointment. Save it again after loading a new listing. Your own wordings are kept here too.

The list, the sent marks, your visit notes, the settings and your wordings go to your other devices through the sync and sit in the backup file. A backup from the standalone Congregation Reminders app loads under the app's Settings, "Bring in a file from the old apps".

The month plan

Every month the district sends its programme as an Excel sheet: the days of the month, where the District Apostle, the Apostle and the District Rector will be, the district's events, and the list of ministers with their codes. Month plan (under More on a phone) opens that file. Type your congregation's name once, the heading of your column in the sheet, then Choose the Excel file. The app reads the days and the ministers on your device; the file goes nowhere. Every Sunday and Wednesday of the month comes up as a service day, and so do the feast days of the Church year on whatever weekday they fall: Good Friday, Easter, Ascension Day, Pentecost and Christmas Day, each named on its row. A public holiday the sheet marks closes the Wednesday service, never a Sunday's or a feast day's, and a day on which the sheet puts the District Rector or the Apostle in your congregation already shows them as holding the service. A Sunday the sheet marks AVT, the Apostle's service transmitted to all congregations, shows AVT as who holds it, since nobody holds it in your congregation; AVT goes into your column and the message too, and you can still choose someone instead.

Tap a service to say who holds it: yourself, one of your priests, the visiting minister, or a name typed in. The same panel has the time (09:00 on a Sunday, 19:30 in the week, both changeable under Settings), a tick for a day without a service, and a line for the priests about that day. Add a service puts a divine service on any other day of the month, with its time and, if you like, a name; a day you added is taken off again from its panel. Your priests' names come from the sheet's ministers' list; under Settings on this tab you write each as the priests should see it, and tell the app your own code, the letter your section's codes start with, if it did not find it by itself. A priest the list does not have you add yourself, and one who has moved you take off. When a public holiday falls on a Wednesday, the Thursday after it comes up as the midweek service by itself, or the day the sheet names; in Christmas week the Wednesday's service does not move, because Christmas Day is that week's service.

Send to the priests gives three things. The Excel file is the district's own file with your congregation's column written in: the code of who holds each service, nothing on a day without one, and the rest of the sheet left as the district made it. The Programme (PDF) is the month on one page, a grid of the weeks with each service, who holds it and the district's events in their days, laid out as the congregation's programme always was. The WhatsApp text is the services and events as a message to paste in the priests' group. On a phone each opens the share sheet; on a PC it is saved.

In the calendar lists what the month could put in your calendar: the services, the district's events, the meetings and the deadlines, each with a tick. Add an event puts in something of your own, such as a service you hold at another congregation; it goes into the calendar and onto the priests' programme. The standard for a new month is set under Settings: services only, services and district events, everything, or nothing until you tick it. Put the plan in Google Calendar makes a feed of the ticked events and shows the steps: once, on the PC, you add the feed's address under "Other calendars → From URL" in Google Calendar, and from then on a calendar named after your congregation sits beside your own, on the phone too. A change in the app reaches it by itself, within about a day, also one an update of the app brings, such as Christmas Day on a Friday; Send now sends it at once. The address is long and random, so only someone who has it sees the plan, and nobody can change it from there. Stop the feed ends it.

The months you opened, your choices and the tab's settings go to your other devices through the sync and sit in the backup file, with the district's file itself, so the Excel file can be written from any of them. Next month at the foot opens the next programme when it comes; a month that is already here is read again and keeps your choices.

Preparing a divine service

The steps follow the order of the Divine Service Guide's article, so you can go through the booklet and the app side by side: the Bible text, the title and message, the introduction, the main points, the context, the summary.

Each open step starts with one line on what to do, and a small ? beside it opens the longer note; so does each Claude button. The notes stand open the first time you open a preparation on a device and fold from then on. Each step folds too. Tap its heading to open or close it; a closed step says in one line what it holds, or "Nothing yet". The bar of steps under the heading (beside the page on a wide screen) jumps to any step: a solid number means the step has something in it, an outlined one that it is still empty, and the lit step is the one on the screen. Open all and Close all are at the end of the bar. The app remembers which steps you left open.

  1. Select the text word in the reader and choose Prepare a service, tap a service of the loaded Guide, or tap "New preparation" in the Prepare tab. Fill in the date, congregation and occasion, and the hymns of the service by number: opening, before the sermon, Holy Communion, closing. The Choir planner's index gives the title as you type (a few words of the title find the number), and the numbers go to the top of the sermon notes and the cue cards.
  2. Step 1, the text word: the Bible text. It can be fetched from a translation or typed in.
  3. Step 2, title and message: the Guide's title and message, then the core thought in your own words, and your notes on the Guide's article.
  4. Step 3, introduction: how you will open the sermon. "Read" shows the Guide's own introduction beside yours.
  5. Step 4, main points: the main points always come from the Divine Service Guide: the parts of its article, in its order, are main point 1, 2, 3 and so on. A preparation taken from the Guide starts with them (each marked "Guide"), and Take the main points from the Guide fetches them again. Under each point there is a box for your own thought on it: a story, a picture, a question, something that struck you. Claude weaves it into the sermon when it builds it (see "Suggest the sermon" below) and asks you first when something is unclear. To put a thought into one point only, tap Weave my thought into this point under it: Claude writes that point again with your thought in it and leaves the rest of the sermon as it is. Where the thought is unclear, it asks you first in a short sheet. The new text waits under the point, with a line on where your thought went; change its words if you like, then tap Use to put it in place of the point's text (Undo brings the old text back), or Remove.
  6. Step 5, context: like the Guide's Context box, the setting of the text, with the key words, the Catechism sections that quote the text, and cross references. Tick the Catechism sections to keep and choose what the sermon notes should carry for them: the full text of each section, the Catechism's own summary, or the number and title only.
  7. Step 6, summary and conclusion: what the text asks of the congregation this week (the application), then the Guide's summary in a few short lines, and the conclusion that leads to the altar.
  8. Step 7, illustrations and experiences: a true experience, brought in from your notes or written here.
  9. Save or print the sermon notes (everything, A4, in the same order as the steps), the sermon itself (the text alone, in reading order and in large clear type: the text word, the introduction, the main points, the application, the summary and the conclusion, with the illustrations) or the cue cards for the altar (upright A5 cards in large type: under each heading, the introduction, each main point, the application and the conclusion, only one line of short cues, "# cue # cue # cue", each in its own strong colour, as on your own cards; a part is never split over two cards). The app takes one cue from each thought of your text; under the rows, Words on the cards has a line per heading where you type your own cues instead, with # between them, and "Fill in" puts the app's cues in the line for you to change (while your own introduction is empty, the Guide's gives the cues). To print the cue cards on A5 cards, open "More settings" in the print window and choose A5 as the paper size. Read shows the sermon or the cue cards on the screen in large type, with the text size behind "Aa" and the screen kept awake. On a phone, "Share" sends the PDF to WhatsApp or e-mail. The headings on the notes follow the language of the app.
  10. The verses the sermon names: where a part of the sermon names a verse, such as "(John 1:14)", the whole verse is written out under that part, with its reference, so you can read it there and need not look it up: under the boxes and under Claude's suggestions in Prepare, on the sermon notes, in "The sermon" on paper and on the screen, in Altar mode, and the voice reads it too. Step 4 also shows the text word itself above the main points. The words come from the translation of the text word (step 1), and the text word as you have it in step 1; when that Bible cannot give a verse (no connection, or an Afrikaans chapter not pasted in yet), it comes from another Bible in the sermon's language, or else from the bundled English, and that Bible's name stands with the verse. Once you paste the Afrikaans chapter in, the verse is fetched again from it the next time you open the preparation. The words are kept with the preparation, so they are there without a connection and on your other device. A verse that is in no Bible (a slip in the reference) is named under the box as "Not found", so you can put it right. A passage longer than six verses stays a reference, to be read from the Bible.
  11. Hear the sermon: under "Print or save", Read aloud has an ElevenLabs voice read the sermon to you, the same text as "The sermon", in English or in Afrikaans, with the headings in the language of the preparation. Under step 2, Hear the Guide reads the Guide's article the same way, in the Guide's language. The recording stays on this device: listen again as often as you like, save it as an MP3, or read it again after a change (the app says when the sermon has changed since). It needs a key of your own under Settings → Read aloud (see "The voice that reads the sermon" below).
  12. Send to a minister: under "Print or save", it makes a small file of the whole preparation (the boxes, the hymns and the words on the cards; not the status, the reflection, the slides or the recording) and opens the phone's share sheet, so it goes by WhatsApp or e-mail; on a PC the file is saved, to send by hand. The other minister opens it in the app and it lands in his Prepare list as his own copy, a draft with "Sent by" and your name at the top, to change as he likes; nothing goes through the site. On an installed Android app, the file in WhatsApp is shared to the app: long-press the document, Share, Study Bible. Anywhere else, Open the file at the foot of the Prepare list picks the saved file. The same preparation sent again replaces the copy, after a question. So a rector hands a service to a priest, or a priest asks the rector to look at his.
  13. Step 8, after the service: a short reflection. It is saved with the preparation.
  14. Step 9, reflection evening: the slides for the evening when the congregation looks back on this service (see "Reflection evening" below).

Everything saves as you type. Use the status (Draft, Ready, Held) to keep the list tidy. After a service's date, its card in the list says "Not marked as held yet", with Mark as held beside it: one tap, and the list clears itself; the message at the bottom can undo it for a few seconds. A service you mark Held is hidden from the lists under Prepare. To see it again, tap Show held services in the list, or search for it. To start a part again, each box has a Clear at its right while it has text, and each step a Clear this step; both can be undone from the message at the bottom for a few seconds. "Change" under step 2 picks another service from the Guide and asks whether to replace the text word, title and message; its context and summary come in too where you have not changed them, and your own notes stay.

Reflection evening

On a reflection evening the congregation looks back on a divine service that was held and talks about it, one question at a time. The app makes the slides for that evening in the Church's blue look: the sermon and its text word, the main points from the Guide, one slide for each question, and a word of thanks to close. Each slide comes with notes for the one who leads the evening.

  1. Tap Reflection evening under Tools in the side menu (on a phone: More, then Reflection evening under Tools). Choose the service, or load the Church's reflection guide there. Evenings with slides come first, under Your slides. You can also open the preparation of the service and tap Make the slides under step 9.
  2. The Church's reflection guide: the one-page PDF for the evening. Tap Load the reflection guide (PDF) and Claude copies its title, text word and questions onto the slides, word for word. The service it looks back on is found among your preparations. If it is not there, it is added and marked as held. Without the PDF the slides take the title and text from the preparation, and the Church's four usual questions.
  3. What the slides say: the language of the slides (English or Afrikaans), the date of the evening, the congregation, the title, the text word and the message. The main points come from the Divine Service Guide, as always: they are never made up. Untick Show the main points on a slide to leave that slide out. What you change here changes the slides, not your preparation.
  4. The questions: one slide for each question, in this order. Change the words, move a question up or down, remove one (Undo brings it back) or add your own. Use the Church's four questions brings the usual four back, in the language of the slides.
  5. The last slide: your own closing words if you like, with the text word to take home.
  6. Notes for the facilitator: for each slide, what to say and do there: the welcome, reading the text word with some background from the Guide, the main points with their main thoughts, how to lead each question, and the close. They are made from the sermon and the Guide. Change anything; empty a box to get the suggestion back. They go into the PowerPoint file as its notes: in PowerPoint's Presenter View only you see them on your laptop while the projector shows the slides. Save the notes as PDF or Print the notes gives them on paper, slide by slide. They run on from page to page without empty space; where a slide goes on over the page, the top of the next page says which slide it is.

Show the slides fills the screen. Tap a small slide to start the show there. On a projector or TV, the arrow keys, the space bar or a presentation clicker go to the next slide, B makes the screen black and Esc ends the show. On a phone or tablet, tap or swipe. Save as PowerPoint gives a file that opens in PowerPoint or Google Slides, where you can still change it. On a phone, Share sends it to WhatsApp or e-mail. Everything you set here is saved with the preparation as you type. In the Prepare list and under Reflection evening, a service with slides shows Slides. Delete these slides, at the bottom, takes away what you changed or loaded for the evening (Undo brings it back); the preparation stays as it is.

Help from Claude (the AI helper)

In Theology, Ask Claude writes the background of a book, a chapter or a passage, in line with the Catechism. It works with a key of your own from Anthropic, under Settings → AI helper (the steps are there): you pay Anthropic only for what you use, usually under 20 US cents an answer, and the key stays on your device, out of any backup. What is sent to Anthropic to answer: the verses or the chapter, the app's own introduction to the book and the Catechism sections that quote it. Anthropic processes it to answer and does not train on it. The answer is a help for study, not Church doctrine.

Claude can also take a few chores off the preparation, with the same key: roughly R10 to read a month's Guide and a few rand for a set of suggestions.

The helper is the minister's own. Under Settings → AI helper, Choose a password. From then on the Guide, the suggestions, the translation of a note and the reflection guide show only on a device where the password was typed, once, and a locked device shows no sign of them: not in Prepare, not in Settings, not on this page. To unlock a device, open Settings there and tap the words AI helper five times: the sheet for the password opens. Lock on this device closes it again, for example before handing the phone to someone. The password travels with the sync as a lock, never as the password itself; a new password asks again on each device. Forgot it? Change the password on a device that is still unlocked, or tap "AI helper" five times and choose I forgot the password: your sync code removes the lock. Anyone else may still Ask Claude in Theology, with a key of their own; the minister's key is never used for that.

  • The Divine Service Guide: in the Prepare tab (and under Church files), Load a Guide (PDF) takes the month's Guide (English or Afrikaans). Claude reads every service out of it: date, Bible text, title, message, the article, the Context box, the Summary and the Catechism sections it cites. The services are then listed in the Prepare tab; tap one and a preparation opens with the text word, title, message, main points, context and summary filled in, each in its own step, and the Catechism sections ticked. Under step 2, Read the article shows the Guide's text while you work. A preparation started in the reader on a text the Guide covers picks up the article by itself; "Take the message from the Guide" attaches one by hand. Loaded Guides stay on your own devices and are not in the backup file; Send this month's Guide passes one to another minister as a file (see Church files).
  • The Guide in English and in Afrikaans: you can load the same month's Guide in both languages. Load one, then tap Load the Afrikaans Guide (PDF) (or the English one) on the Guide's card. The app links the two: each service to the same service in the other language. The card then has a switch, English | Afrikaans, to show either list, and a preparation shows in both. A preparation follows the Guide you start it from, and its sermon is in that language, so Claude's suggestions come in that language too (you can change it at the top of the preparation). Under step 2, The Guide in moves a preparation to the other language: wherever a box still holds the Guide's own words (the text, title, message, main points, context and summary), the other language's words come in, and what you wrote yourself stays. Read it in Afrikaans (or English) under the article shows the other Guide's words, to compare.
  • Break down the article: under step 2, once a preparation is linked to a service in the Guide, Claude takes the Guide's article apart sentence by sentence (two or three sentences together where one alone is too thin), under the Guide's own headings. For each piece it gives the Guide's words, what they say in plain words, what they give the sermon, and, where one truly brings depth, a Bible passage or a Catechism section with a line on what it adds. "The thread" at the top says what the whole article drives at. Use on a piece puts the plain words and the insight into "Notes from the Guide" (with the Guide's sentence above them, so you find it in the booklet); Use on a passage puts it into Cross references; Tick on a section ticks it under step 5, so it prints with the sermon notes; Use on the thread puts it into Core thought. Under Print or save in the panel, Piece by piece gives the whole breakdown as a study sheet of its own, and The article in plain words gives the article as one piece in plain words only, under the Guide's headings and with its paragraphs, to read in one go; each with Save as PDF, Print and, on a phone, Share. The switch Piece by piece | In plain words under the thread shows the article the same two ways on the screen, so you can read it as one piece in the app, in your theme and in Easy-read. The plain words and insights come in the language chosen for the sermon, even when the Guide is in the other language.
  • Help me understand the text: under step 5, Claude answers the questions about the text (who speaks to whom, why, what came before and after, the setting, the meaning), explains the key words, names the Catechism sections that belong with the text and the message, gives cross references in line with the message, and offers two or three sermon thoughts with an application. It reads the text and its chapter, the Guide's article when one is attached, and the Catechism sections (their text, when it is loaded on this device). Every suggestion has a Use button that adds it to the matching box; nothing goes into your notes until you tap it. The suggestions are kept with the preparation, so they do not cost again when you open it later; "Ask again" does. Where the Guide's article is attached, the "sermon thoughts" are the Guide's own main points, in its order, each with what the text gives it; Claude adds no point of its own.
  • Suggest the sermon: under step 4, Claude proposes how the sermon is best built: an introduction, the main points, the application, the conclusion that leads to the altar, and illustrations, each with a line on why it works for this text and message. The main points are the Guide's: the parts of the attached article, in the Guide's order and under its headings (shown under each point when Claude puts the heading in the sermon's language), written out in plain words for your people; a point Claude adds of its own is left out and the panel says so, and a point it leaves out stays in the Guide's words. The introduction, application, conclusion and illustrations are Claude's, but each stands on those points. It builds on what you have written already in the other steps, on the article and its breakdown, the ticked Catechism sections and your own experience and illustration notes, and writes each part as you could speak it. Use puts a part into its box (the illustrations go under step 7); on a main point it fills the box of that point, with Undo; "Use all" fills all the points in the Guide's order, asking first when yours are not empty. Without the Guide's article Claude proposes no main points and says they need the Guide, unless you have written them yourself (then it fills them out) or the service is a funeral or a wedding, which the Guide has no article for. Your own thoughts: where you have written a thought under a main point, Claude reads your thoughts first, before it builds anything, to see where each one fits. Where a thought is unclear, or could go two ways, or Claude needs a detail to use it well, it asks you in a short sheet; answer in a few words and tap "Build the sermon", or "Not now" to answer later (the question stays under the point, with a box for your answer). The sermon is then built with every thought woven in, in your words where they are good, and the outline says under "Your thoughts" where each one went and how. Your answers are kept with the preparation, so a thought is only read again when you change it or answer something. Save as PDF and Print beside "Suggest again" give the whole outline as one sermon to read, with each illustration under the point it serves.
  • Change the words first: tap any suggestion (a piece of the breakdown, an answer, a part of the outline), or the pencil beside it, and its words open in a box to change. Use then puts in your words, and "Done" closes the box. What you change is kept with the preparation and goes into the study sheet and the next "Suggest the sermon"; the Guide's own words in the breakdown stay as they are.
  • For the people in front of you: at the top of a preparation, under the date and the congregation, you say who is listening: the age group (all ages, children, young people, young adults, families, mostly older members), a line about the people (their work, their language, what they are going through, how many), how much humour the sermon may carry, and whether the suggestions should come in English or Afrikaans. Claude shapes the words, the pictures, the examples and the application for them. For an ordinary Sunday, leave it as it is; the age group and the line about the people come along to your next preparation in the same congregation, and the line also heads the printed sermon notes. There is never humour in a funeral or a service for the departed. A light moment Claude thinks fits is offered on its own under "Suggest the sermon", so you can take it or leave it.
  • In your words: every suggestion comes in short sentences and everyday words, so it is easy to read on a phone and to say from the pulpit; where a Church or Bible word is needed, Claude says in plain words what it means. Under Settings → AI helper → How you preach you set the usual humour and can write a few lines about your own way of preaching, which Claude follows in every suggestion.

What is sent to Anthropic to answer: the Guide PDF or the reflection guide when you load one, the text word and its chapter, the Catechism sections that belong to it, your own notes on that preparation, who is listening and your note on how you preach. Anthropic processes it to answer and does not train on it; nothing of it is put on the public site. The suggestions are a starting point. The sermon remains yours.

Keys for the licensed translations

Two services license Bible translations to apps, and both give a free key for personal, non-commercial use. API.Bible: open scripture.api.bible/signup, create an application and copy its key; it carries the NKJV. YouVersion Platform: register at platform.youversion.com, create an app and copy its app key; YouVersion carries the Afrikaans Bibles. Which translations appear depends on the licences each service has granted your key. YouVersion also shows the Bibles it carries in your languages that are not licensed to your app yet, marked "licence needed": tap Licence next to one to see which agreement covers it, accept that agreement on platform.youversion.com, then check the key again. The voice that reads the sermon

ElevenLabs makes the voice that reads a sermon aloud. Open elevenlabs.io, create an account or sign in, choose "API keys" under your name at the bottom left and create a key (leave its access as it is; the key is shown once). Paste it under Settings → Read aloud and tap "Check key": it lists the voices of your account, and you choose one for English and one for Afrikaans, hear a sample of each, and set the speed. Under "Feeling" you choose how much feeling the voice puts in (Steady, Natural or Lively), and under "Tone" the words that tell it how to read, such as gentle and reassuring: the app puts them before each paragraph for the newer ElevenLabs voices, and they count as characters too. Without a choice, the app reads with Jerome's own voices, cloned at ElevenLabs, one for English and one for Afrikaans; with another account's key they fall back to George, a voice every account has. Any voice can read Afrikaans; for a South African sound, add an Afrikaans voice from the Voice Library at elevenlabs.io to your voices. You pay ElevenLabs for the characters it reads: a sermon of about 1,500 words is about 8,000 characters; the free plan gives 10,000 a month, the Starter plan (about $6 a month) 75,000. A recording is kept on the device, so listening again costs nothing. Only the text of the sermon goes to ElevenLabs; it keeps the recordings in your account's history, where you can delete them. The key stays on this device and is never in a backup.

Sync between your devices

Your PC and phone can keep each other the same. Under Settings → Sync between your devices, tap Turn on sync on the first device: the app makes a code of 20 letters and numbers and shows it with a QR square. On the other device, open the same screen, tap I have a code and type the code in, or point the phone's camera at the square (if the app is on your home screen, open it from there and type the code, so that copy is the one that joins). From then on both devices keep each other up to date on their own: notes, preparations, settings, pasted text, the Catechism text, the Guides and what Claude wrote in Theology travel across within a few seconds of opening the app or making a change. Something you delete on one device goes on the other too; when the same note or preparation was changed on both before they could sync, the newer change wins.

Everything is locked with your code on the device before it is sent. The server, a small part of the app on Vercel, keeps only the locked copy and cannot read it, and nobody can find or open it without the code, so keep the code where you keep your passwords: Show the code shows it again any time (a locked device does not show it: unlock the helper first). Your keys for API.Bible, YouVersion and Anthropic travel too, locked in the same way, unless you untick Carry my keys too. The look of the app (theme and text size) stays per device. Stop syncing on this device keeps everything on the device and leaves the copy on the server for your other devices; Delete the copy on the server removes it for good. Erase everything on this device under About touches only that device.

Good to know: if the app is removed from a device, or the browser clears its data, everything on that device is gone with it. Install the app again, tap I have a code and type your code, and it all comes back; a backup file (below) is the second way back. An update never needs that: the app says when a newer version is ready, and Update now keeps everything.

Backup and devices

Notes, preparations and what Claude wrote in Theology live in the browser on each device. Under Settings → Backup, Save backup file makes one file with everything; keep it in your Google Drive, also when sync is on: it is your own copy, in a file you can see. Load backup file on another device merges it in: newer copies win and nothing is deleted. Clearing the browser's site data erases the app's storage, so save a backup first.

Offline and home screen

The app keeps every chapter you open. To have the whole Bible ready without a connection, use Download all texts for offline in Settings once. The Bible then stays on the device, also when the app updates, and the search for a verse works without a connection. Put the app on the home screen to open it like an app: the first-time setup and Settings → Offline offer Add to Home Screen where the browser allows it, and the browser's own menu has it otherwise.

When a newer version of the app is on the site, a bar at the bottom says so: tap Update now. To look straight away, use Check for a newer version under Settings → About.

Die Studiebybel is 'n persoonlike hulpmiddel om die Skrif te lees, jou eie gedagtes daaroor te bewaar, te sien waar die Kategismus van die Nuwe Apostoliese Kerk na 'n vers verwys, en 'n godsdiens uit 'n tekswoord voor te berei. Alles wat jy skryf, bly op jou eie toestel. Daar is geen rekening en geen bediener nie.

Begin hier

'n Nuwe toestel vra hierdie vyf dinge self die eerste keer dat die app oopmaak; dit is dieselfde lys. Verstellings → Oor die app het die vyf stappe weer, onder Stel hierdie toestel weer op.

  1. Die taal. EN of AF boaan die app: die skerms, die knoppies, hierdie hulpblad en die boeknames.
  2. Jou naam en gemeente. Die naam kom in die voetstuk van die preeknotas, en die gemeente word by elke nuwe voorbereiding ingevul (Verstellings → Jy).
  3. 'n Sinkronisasiekode, of 'n nuwe begin. As jy die Studiebybel reeds op 'n ander toestel gebruik, tik sy kode in (Verstellings → Sinkroniseer → Wys die kode, op daardie toestel) en jou notas en voorbereidings kom na hierdie een oor. Anders begin jy nuut; 'n kode kan later ingetik word.
  4. Kerklêers. Die Kategismus, die altaarboekie, die handboeke en die maand se Handleiding is die Kerk se eie en nie deel van die app nie: laai elkeen een keer uit sy lêer onder Kerklêers. 'n Ampsdraer wat hulle het, kan die lêers uit sy app vir jou stuur.
  5. Voeg by tuisskerm. Die app bied dit in die laaste stap en onder Verstellings → Vanlyn aan; waar die blaaier niks aanbied nie, het sy eie kieslys dit. Van die tuisskerm af maak die app soos enige ander oop, werk sonder die netwerk, en wat jy bewaar, word veilig gehou.

Vind jou pad

Die app maak op Sondag oop: die volgende godsdiens, met die datum, die tekswoord, die titel en hoe ver jou voorbereiding is, en een tik na elke ding wat jy op die dag gebruik: die preekkaarte, die preek, die altaarkaart van die Liturgie en die tekswoord in groot letters. Op die dag self sit Altaarmodus op die kaart die preekkaarte en die altaarkaart op een volskerm, 'n veeg uit mekaar, met die preek daarna wanneer dit geskryf is, in groot letters en met die skerm wat aan bly; die ✕ bo bring jou terug. Sondagpakket onder die kaart druk die preekkaarte, met die gesangnommers bo-aan die eerste, en die altaarkaart as een PDF van regop A5-bladsye, in een slag; op A4-papier kies 2 bladsye per vel in die drukvenster. 'n Godsdiens uit 'n gelaaide Handleiding vir Godsdienste wat nog geen voorbereiding het nie, is ook daar, met een tik om dit te begin. Ná die godsdiens, tik Merk as gehou en die volgende godsdiens neem sy plek in; die twee godsdienste daarna staan onder die kaart.

Diensrekord. Ná die diens, tik op Diensrekord (Rekord in 'n foon se balk). Dit maak die rekord oop van die godsdiens wat pas gehou is: vandag s'n, of anders die laaste een. Watter godsdiens bo-aan gaan na 'n ander; die Sondagkaart se Diensrekord en die voorbereiding se Druk-stap maak dit ook oop. NACSA se vorm is op een skerm: die gemeente, datum, tekswoord en soort diens kom uit die voorbereiding, die gesange en jou naam is ingevul, die bywoning en die offergawes tel hulself op, en daar is plek vir die Apostel se verseëlings en ordenings, die opmerkings, wie die rekord voltooi het en wie dit op MIS vasgelê het. Wat jy tik, word soos jy gaan bewaar. In die gesangbokse het die sleutelbord letters: tik die boek voor die nommer, A 104 (Afrikaans), E 104 (Engels) of HC 12 (die Hymn Collection). Onder Die vorm sien jy jou rekord op NACSA se papiervorm, soos die kantoor dit ken (in Engels, soos die papier). Stuur as 'n prent stuur dit soos 'n foto van die vorm: kies WhatsApp, dan die distrik se groep. Stuur as PDF doen dieselfde met 'n PDF, en Twee op een A4 (PDF) gee twee vorms langs mekaar op een vel, soos die papier. Onder Die boodskap maak Stuur na WhatsApp WhatsApp oop met die rekord as getikte reëls, en Kopieer kopieer hulle.

Op 'n rekenaar is elke deel van die app in die blou kieslys links; Sitplekke, Koor, Herinneringe, MIS en Besinningsaand staan daar saam onder die opskrif Hulpmiddels. Op 'n foon hou die balk onderaan Bybel, Voorberei, Sondag, Notas en Rekord (die diensrekord), en 'n sesde oortjie, Meer, lys die res: Handboeke, Teologie, Maandplan, die Hulpmiddels onder hul opskrif, Kerklêers en Verstellings. Terwyl een daarvan oop is, bly Meer aan, sodat een tik daarop die lys terugbring. Die app maak oop op Sondag, die volgende godsdiens.

Verstellings is 'n lys van afdelings, elk met 'n reël oor wat daarin is; tik op een om dit op sy eie oop te maak, en ‹ Verstellings daarbo bring die lys terug. Die afdelings wat 'n nuwe ampsdraer nodig het, kom eerste, onder Begin hier: Taal, Jy, Wat ek gebruik, Sinkroniseer, Kerklêers en Vertalings. Dan wat by hierdie toestel hoort: Voorkoms, Rugsteun, Vanlyn, die lêers van die ou programme en Oor die app. Die sleutels vir die stem en vir Claude kom laaste. Waar hierdie blad "Verstellings → Taal" sê, bedoel dit daardie ry.

Wat ek gebruik, onder Verstellings → Begin hier, is 'n merklys van die vyf hulpmiddels: Maandplan, Sitplekke, Koor, Herinneringe en Besinningsaand. Wat jy ontmerk, verdwyn uit die kieslys op 'n rekenaar en die Meer-bladsy op 'n foon, op al jou toestelle; niks word uitgevee nie, en 'n merkie bring dit terug. 'n Diaken se foon hoef nie die voorganger en die dirigent se hulpmiddels te wys nie.

Handboeke is die deur na die Kerk se boeke: die Kategismus, die Geloofsbelydenis, die Liturgie (die woorde by die altaar) en die handboeke vir ampsdraers, elk 'n ry in sy lys. Terwyl die Kategismus of die Liturgie oop is, bly Handboeke op 'n rekenaar aan, en Meer op 'n foon, sodat een tik daarop die lys terugbring.

Kerklêers, in die kieslys op 'n rekenaar en onder Meer op 'n foon, is waar die Kerk se eie tekste gelaai word, elk een keer uit sy eie lêer: die teks van die Kategismus (uit die Kategismus se PDF), die altaarboekie in Engels en in Afrikaans, die handboeke vir ampsdraers en die Handleiding vir Godsdienste. 'n Ry per lêer sê of dit op hierdie toestel is, met een Laai-knoppie en, sodra dit gelaai is, Verwyder. Wat gelaai is, bereik jou ander toestelle deur die sinkronisasie en is nie in die rugsteunlêer nie.

Die Handleiding se ry werk vir elke ampsdraer. Waar die KI-helper ontsluit is, laat Laai 'n Handleiding (PDF) Claude die maand se boekie lees; dan maak Stuur hierdie maand se Handleiding 'n klein lêer daarvan, in elke taal wat gelaai is, en maak op 'n foon die deelblad oop met WhatsApp daarin (op 'n rekenaar word die lêer gestoor, om met die hand te stuur). Die ampsdraer wat dit kry, tik hier op Laai 'n Handleiding-lêer, of op die Handleiding se kaart onder Diens Voorbereiding, en kies die lêer: die godsdienste, die artikels en die hoofpunte is dan ook op daardie toestel, sonder 'n sleutel en sonder Claude. Op 'n foon waar die app geïnstalleer is, lys WhatsApp se Deel op die lêer die app self, en die Handleiding laai dadelik. Die Handleiding gaan van een ampsdraer se toestel na 'n ander s'n en nooit deur die webwerf nie.

MIS, onder Hulpmiddels, maak die Kerk se eie MIS-webwerf oop. In die geïnstalleerde app maak dit bo-oor die app oop: op 'n foon met 'n Done of ✕ bo-aan om terug te kom, op 'n rekenaar in die app se venster met 'n balk bo-aan waarvan die ✕ die app terugbring. In 'n blaaier maak dit in sy eie oortjie oop. Meld daar aan soos altyd; die app sien of bewaar nooit jou wagwoord nie. Die MIS kan nie binne die app se eie blad sit nie: dit meld jou met 'n koekie aan, wat 'n blaaier binne 'n ander webwerf se blad terughou, so die aanmelding het deurgegaan, maar die MIS kon dit nie sien nie.

Lees

  • Maak 'n gedeelte oop: kies die boek en hoofstuk bo-aan. Op 'n foon is die balk een ry: tik op die hoofstuk se naam (Psalm 23) om 'n boek en 'n hoofstuk uit 'n rooster te kies, met die hoofstukke voor en ná bo-aan; die balk skuif weg soos jy af lees en kom terug wanneer jy op rol. Die ‹- en ›-knoppies blaai die hoofstuk, bo-aan op 'n rekenaar en aan die einde van die hoofstuk op 'n foon; op 'n rekenaar doen die linker- en regterpyltjie dieselfde.
  • Soek 'n vers: die boks bo-aan (op 'n foon agter die vergrootglas) neem 'n plek of woorde. Tik 'n plek soos Ps 130:7, Lukas 8:16 of 1 Kor 13 en die hoofstuk maak daar oop. Engelse en Afrikaanse boekname werk albei, en 'n klein glips in 'n naam, soos Jhon 3:16, word verstaan. Of tik 'n paar woorde wat jy onthou, soos shepherd want. Die app lys die verse wat hulle het, met die verse wat die woorde naby mekaar het, eerste. Tik op 'n vers om dit oop te maak; "Terug na die verse" bo die hoofstuk gaan terug na die lys. 'n Spelfout word reggestel waar dit kan, en die bladsy sê watter woord gesoek is. Sit woorde in "aanhalingstekens" om hulle langs mekaar te vind, presies soos getik.
  • Wat deursoek word: die vertaling waarop die leser gestel is, en die bladsy sê altyd watter een. Tik die woorde in die taal van daardie vertaling. Die KJV en die WEB word op die toestel deursoek. Die eerste soektog gaan haal die hele Bybel, wat 'n oomblik neem en 'n verbinding nodig het, tensy jy dit met "Laai alle tekste af vir vanlyn gebruik" gestoor het. Daarna werk die soektog sonder 'n verbinding. 'n Teks wat jy ingeplak het, word deursoek in die hoofstukke wat jy ingeplak het. Die NKJV word deur API.Bible met jou sleutel deursoek, dus het dit 'n verbinding nodig; wanneer dit nie werk nie, word die KJV deursoek en die bladsy sê so.
  • Vertalings: die King James Version (met die Apokriewe) en die World English Bible is ingebou en werk vanlyn. Kies die vertaling in die balk bo-aan.
  • Die Aa-knoppie hou die leeskeuses: die teksgrootte (die grootste, "Kansel", is bedoel vir lees terwyl jy staan), die voorkoms van die app (Stelsel, Wit, Papier of Donker), die letters en spasiëring ("Maklik-lees" gebruik eenvoudige letters met meer ruimte tussen hulle en tussen die reëls, in die hele app, wat baie mense met disleksie makliker vind), die taal van die app, "Vergelyk", wat 'n tweede vertaling onder elke vers wys, en "Langsaan", wat dieselfde hoofstuk van BibleSA, die Bybelgenootskap van Suid-Afrika se gratis webwerf met die Afrikaanse Bybels, in sy eie venster langs die app oopmaak; solank daardie venster oop bly, volg dit jou van hoofstuk tot hoofstuk. (BibleSA laat nie toe dat sy bladsye binne 'n ander webwerf gewys word nie, dus is 'n venster die naaste wat die app kan kom.)
  • Jou eie teks: vir 'n Bybelteks wat jy self mag gebruik, kies sy weergawe in die leser se vertalingslys (sy kort naam, soos AFR83, onder "Plak dit self in"). 'n Leë hoofstuk wys Maak oop op BibleSA en Plak die hoofstuk: kopieer die hoofstuk daar, met sy versnommers, kom terug en tik Plak; dit word van die knipbord geneem en dadelik gehou, met Ontdoen in die boodskap. Dit bly net in hierdie blaaier en het sy eie stoorlêer onder Verstellings → Vertalings → "My geplakte teks".
  • NKJV en Afrikaans: hierdie is kopieregbeskerm en kan nie ingebou word nie. Voeg jou eie gratis sleutel vir API.Bible of die YouVersion Platform in Verstellings by om hulle binne die app te lees (sien hieronder), of gebruik die skakels onder elke hoofstuk om dieselfde hoofstuk op YouVersion, op BibleSA of op BibleGateway oop te maak.
  • Merke: 'n pers C beteken die Kategismus verwys na daardie vers; tik daarop om te sien watter afdelings. 'n Pen beteken jy het 'n nota oor daardie vers.

Voorkoms

Die app dra die Kerk se blou en wit, soos die skyfies: die blou kopstrook met die embleem op 'n foon, die blou kantkieslys op 'n rekenaar, en elke letter in Montserrat. Onder Verstellings → Voorkoms (en onder die Aa-knoppie in die leser) kies jy die tema: Wit is die blou en wit van die skyfies, Papier hou die blou maar gee die bladsye 'n warm roomkleur, Donker is om snags te lees, en Stelsel is Wit bedags en Donker snags, soos jou foon of rekenaar ingestel is. Dieselfde plek stel die teksgrootte, die letters en spasiëring (Montserrat, of Maklik-lees: gewone letters in die hele app met meer ruimte tussen hulle) en of boekname in Engels of Afrikaans gewys word. Elke toestel hou sy eie keuse.

Notas

  • Tik op 'n vers (of 'n paar) in die leser en kies Nota. Gee dit 'n soort: 'n gedagte, 'n ervaring, 'n leerskakel, 'n illustrasie, 'n preekidee of 'n vraag. Etikette is opsioneel.
  • Die Notas-oortjie lys alles, en soek volgens woord, etiket of verwysing.
  • Luister: lees 'n nota hardop met die stem wat in jou foon of rekenaar ingebou is, gratis en dadelik, in die nota se taal (Engels of Afrikaans, as die toestel so 'n stem het; Verstellings → Lees hardop sê watter dit het). Daar kan jy eerder ElevenLabs se goedkoopste stem vir notas kies; daardie opname bly op die toestel, dus kos dit niks om weer te luister nie.
  • Elke kaart wys eers die vers, die soort en die nota se eie woorde; die datum, Luister en Wysig staan daaronder, sodat 'n lys van vyftig notas soos 'n bladsy afgelees kan word.
  • Engels | Afrikaans: 'n nota wat albei tale het, wys onder sy woorde 'n EN | AF-skakelaar: wissel tussen die twee, ook sonder internet en op jou ander toestelle. Wysig verander die taal wat jy sien. Bybelverse wat uit 'n vertaling aangehaal is, bly soos hulle is. 'n Nota in een taal wys geen skakelaar nie.
  • Om 'n nota ook in die ander taal te hê, maak Wysig oop en tik Vertaal na Afrikaans (of Engels): Claude vertaal dit een keer (dit het jou Anthropic-sleutel nodig, onder Verstellings → KI-helper). Die ander taal bly soos dit is, en sê so as die nota ná die vertaling verander het.
  • Ervarings en illustrasies kan met een tik in 'n preekvoorbereiding ingevoeg word, in die voorbereiding se taal as die nota dit het.

Kategismus

Kategismus, die eerste ry onder Handboeke, lys die Kategismus se hoofstukke en afdelings. Maak 'n afdeling oop om die Bybelverse te sien wat dit aanhaal. Die teks van die afdelings is die Kerk se kopiereg, dus is dit nie deel van die openbare app nie: laai dit een keer op elke toestel onder Verstellings → Kategismus, uit die lêer catechism-text.json wat uit die Kategismus-PDF gemaak word (python3 bible/tools/build_catechism.py catechism.txt catechism-toc.txt --text catechism-text.json, waar catechism.txt die PDF as teks uit pdftotext -layout is). Dit bly in hierdie blaaier op hierdie toestel en is nie in die rugsteunlêer nie.

Met die teks gelaai kan jy elke afdeling onder Kategismus lees, met die boek se eie samevatting bo-aan en "sien 4.2"-skakels tussen afdelings; die hele Kategismus per woord deursoek; op die C by 'n vers tik om die samevatting te sien van elke afdeling wat dit aanhaal; en 'n afdeling onder 'n godsdiensvoorbereiding met "Lees" oopmaak. Daarsonder skakel elke afdeling na dieselfde teks by nak.org. Die Kategismus se teks en indeks is in Engels, soek dus daarin met Engelse woorde. Met die teks gelaai maak Druk op 'n afdeling "Druk of stoor" oop: die afdeling, of die afdeling met dié daaronder, as 'n PDF in groot duidelike letters, met Stoor as PDF, Druk en, op 'n foon, Deel.

Vra die Kategismus: waar die KI-helper ontsluit is en die teks gelaai is, neem 'n blokkie onder die opskrif 'n vraag in jou eie woorde. Claude lees die afdelings wat daarby pas en antwoord daaruit in gewone woorde, met die afdelings by die naam, elk 'n skakel, sodat jy kan lees wat die Kategismus self sê. Die antwoord word nie bewaar nie. Dieselfde vraag kan onder Handboeke gevra word, waar die Kategismus saam met die handboeke gelees word.

Teologie

Teologie gee die agtergrond by die Bybel op drie vlakke: die verse wat jy kies, die hoofstuk, en die boek met sy skrywer. Op 'n rekenaar het dit sy eie plek in die kantkieslys; op 'n foon is dit onder Meer. Jy kom ook daar uit die leser: die Teologie-knoppie langs die hoofstuk se titel maak daardie hoofstuk oop, en nadat jy op verse getik het, maak Teologie in die balk onderaan 'n blad oor daardie verse oop, met die hoofstuk en die boek een tik weg.

Elke boek van die Bybel het 'n kort ingeboude inleiding, die latere geskrifte van die Ou Testament (die Apokriewe) ingesluit, in Engels en in Afrikaans: wie dit geskryf het, wanneer, vir wie en waarom, sy groot temas, hoe dit opgebou is, sy sleutelverse en hoe dit na Christus wys. Die boeke is gegroepeer soos die Kategismus hulle groepeer, en by elke boek, hoofstuk en gedeelte staan die Kategismus-afdelings wat dit aanhaal. Die inleidings is vir die app in eenvoudige woorde geskryf: 'n hulp vir studie, nie die leer van die Kerk nie.

Met jou Anthropic-sleutel (sien "Hulp van Claude" hieronder) skryf Vra Claude die agtergrond van die verse, die hoofstuk of die boek: wat dit in eenvoudige woorde sê, die agtergrond en skrywer, die sleutelwoorde, wat die Kerk daaroor leer, hoe ander Christene dit lees (elke lesing gemerk as in lyn met die Nuwe Apostoliese leer, anders as dit, of deur die Kategismus oopgelaat), hoe dit na Christus wys, ander gedeeltes, en 'n gedagte vir vandag. Met die Kategismus-teks op die toestel gelaai, sê Claude uit die Kategismus self wat die Kerk leer; daarsonder noem Claude net die afdelings. Dit neem omtrent 'n minuut, en die koste wys saam met die antwoord. Wat Claude geskryf het, word op die toestel bewaar, gaan in die rugsteunlêer en reis saam met die sinkronisasie. Vra weer skryf dit opnuut en hou die vroeëre antwoord onder die nuwe een: tik daarop om dit te lees, of tik op Sit hierdie een eerste. Niks wat Claude geskryf het, verdwyn tensy jy op Verwyder tik nie; dit vra eers en neem net daardie een antwoord weg (Ontdoen bring dit terug).

Engels | Afrikaans: elke antwoord het bo-aan 'n EN | AF-skakelaar. Tik op die ander taal en Claude vertaal die antwoord een keer (die koste wys wanneer dit klaar is). Daarna wissel jy tussen die twee, ook sonder internet en op jou ander toestelle, en die lys onder Teologie wys elke antwoord in die app se taal as dit een het. As die antwoord in die eerste taal later verander, sê die vertaling so en bied Vertaal weer aan; die vroeëre vertaling bly onder die nuwe een. Vra weer en Verwyder werk op die taal wat jy sien.

Onder wat Claude geskryf het, hou Stoor dit alles alles wat die blad wys: die verse (by 'n boek, sy inleiding), alles wat Claude geskryf het en die Kategismus-afdelings. Stoor as PDF gee 'n lêer om te hou, byvoorbeeld in jou Google Drive, of om te stuur. Druk druk dit, en op 'n foon stuur Deel dit reguit na 'n ander app. Stoor as 'n nota sit alles in een van jou notas, as 'n Leer-nota by daardie plek, en jy kan dit nog verander voordat jy dit stoor.

Liturgie

Liturgie hou die Kerk se altaarboekie, Liturgie vir Altaar Gebruik: die woorde wat by die altaar gesê word in die godsdiens en in elke seënhandeling (opname, konfirmasie, 'n huwelik, 'n begrafnis, die seën voor 'n geboorte, 'n huweliksherdenking, die Heilige Doop), een kaart per handeling, soos die boekie dit druk: die etiket links, die woorde regs, die boekie se aantekeninge in skuinsdruk en sy lyste. Dit is 'n ry onder Handboeke, op 'n rekenaar en op 'n foon, en op 'n foon sit dit ook bo-aan Voorberei.

Die boekie is die Kerk se eie, dus is dit nie deel van die app nie. Laai sy PDF een keer onder Liturgie: Laai die Engelse boekie (PDF) of Laai die Afrikaanse boekie (PDF), of albei. Die app lees die kaarte uit die lêer op die toestel self (geen Claude, geen sleutel, 'n paar sekondes), hou hulle in hierdie blaaier en stuur hulle deur die sinkronisasie na jou ander toestelle, dus is een laai genoeg. Hulle is nie in die rugsteunlêer nie. Verwyder haal 'n boekie weer van jou toestelle af (Ontdoen bring dit terug), en die PDF kan enige tyd opnuut gelaai word; Laai 'n nuwer lêer vervang 'n boekie.

Die kaarte wys in die app se taal, of in die ander taal as net daardie boekie gelaai is, en 'n kaart het 'n EN | AF-skakelaar as albei gelaai is. Aa stel die teksgrootte, dieselfde groottes as in die leser, tot Kansel. Terwyl 'n kaart oop is, hou die foon sy skerm aan, sodat die woorde by die altaar bly.

Druk op 'n kaart maak "Druk of stoor" oop: die kaart as 'n PDF, die etiket links en die woorde regs soos die boekie dit druk, op A4 of op A5, die grootte van die kaarte by die altaar. 'n Kaart word op een bladsy ingepas, in die grootste letters wat daar pas, en die reël onder die papier wat jy kies, sê so. Net 'n kaart wat te lank is om in daardie grootte gelees te word, neem 'n tweede bladsy; 'n kaart wat op A5 twee bladsye nodig het, pas dikwels op een bladsy van A4. A5, een kant hou so 'n kaart tog op een kant van 'n A5-kaart, in kleiner letters, en die reël sê watter grootte hulle is (8 punt vir die Godsdiens). Wanneer 'n nuwer weergawe van die app op die webwerf is, sê 'n balk onderaan so: tik Dateer nou op. Om dadelik te kyk, gebruik Kyk vir 'n nuwer weergawe onder Verstellings → Oor die app.
