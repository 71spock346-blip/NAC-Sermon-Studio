1 Foreword
This handbook describes what a helper does in the fellowship, and how a helper
can prepare for the ordinary tasks of a week. It is short on purpose, so that it
can be read in one sitting.
Nothing in it replaces a conversation. Where a helper is unsure, the helper asks
the leader of the fellowship, who will gladly explain.
2 Helping
Helping means giving time and attention to the people of the fellowship, and
doing so in a way that they can rely on.
2.1 What a helper does
A helper takes care of the following:
 Welcoming people at the door before a meeting
 Keeping promises, and saying so when one cannot
be kept
 Passing on news to the leader of the fellowship
 Looking after the room after a meeting
Helpers are not expected to do everything at once. The leader of the fellowship
shares the tasks out among the helpers who are present.

2.2 Visiting a member
Members who cannot come to the meetings are visited at home. A visit is a sign
that the fellowship has not forgotten them, and it is often the high point of
their week.
The visit
A visit is arranged beforehand with a telephone call to the member’s home1 so
that the member can expect it. The helper brings news of the fellowship, listens
more than he or she talks, and ends the visit with a short prayer if the member
wishes it.
A visit should not last longer than an hour. Where the member is unwell, half an
hour is enough.

1 Or with a message to a relative, where the member has no telephone.

3 Meetings
3.1 Planning a meeting
A meeting is planned a week ahead, so that everyone who should be there can be
told in time.
3.1.1 Who to invite
Everyone who belongs to the fellowship is invited. Guests are welcome when a
member brings them along and introduces them to the leader of the fellowship.
3.2 After the meeting
The helpers tidy the room, close the windows and switch off the lights. The
last helper to leave locks the door and keeps the key until the next meeting.

4 Words of thanks
The fellowship thanks every helper for the time given. Without the helpers there
would be no meetings, no visits and no welcome at the door.
